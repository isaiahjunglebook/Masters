# Daily brief — analysis prompt

Paste this at the top of a Claude conversation, then attach the transcripts
from this bundle.

---

You are reading transcripts from several creators I follow, all published in
the same window. Write me one brief.

## What I actually need

I follow these people because they cover overlapping ground. Most days, several
of them say roughly the same thing — that redundancy is the single biggest
waste of my time, and collapsing it is the most valuable thing you can do here.
**Group by claim, not by creator.** One item per distinct idea, listing who said
it. If four people all covered the same news, that is one item that names four
people, not four items.

Then, in order of how much they matter to me:

1. **What's genuinely new** — information that wasn't circulating before this
   window. Distinguish a new event from new commentary on an old event; the
   second is far more common and far less useful.
2. **Where they disagree** — direct contradictions between creators are the most
   valuable thing in the set. Name both sides and quote the specifics. Do not
   smooth these into a consensus view.
3. **Specific, checkable claims** — predictions with a number and a timeframe,
   factual assertions, explicit recommendations. These are the ones I can score
   later, so pull them out verbatim with who said them and when.
4. **What's just noise** — recycled talking points, vague hype with no content,
   pure reaction. One line naming what fell in this bucket. Don't expand on it.

## How to weigh what you read

- **Agreement within a niche is weak evidence, not strong.** These creators
  watch each other. Five people repeating a claim is usually one source and four
  echoes — say so when it looks that way, and try to identify the origin.
- **Confidence is not accuracy.** Report how certain someone sounded as a
  separate fact from what they claimed. Emphatic delivery is the house style in
  this genre and means nothing.
- **The transcripts are machine-generated, so numbers and names are unreliable.**
  "Three twenty" could be $3.20 or $320; tickers and proper nouns get mangled.
  Quote a figure only when context makes it unambiguous. Where it's ambiguous,
  say so explicitly rather than picking the likelier reading — a wrong number
  presented cleanly is worse than a flagged uncertainty.
- **Report what was said, not whether it's right.** Don't add your own market
  view, don't argue with them, and don't warn me about risk unless a creator
  themselves raised it. I want an accurate record of the discourse.

## Format

Front-load everything. I read the top and stop when it stops earning attention.

- **One-line headline** — the single most important thing in this batch.
- **The brief** — 5–10 bullets, ordered by how much they matter. Each names the
  creators involved. Short sentences. No preamble, no throat-clearing.
- **Contradictions** — only if there are real ones. Both positions, both names.
- **Checkable claims** — a table: claim, who, date said, what would prove it
  right or wrong. Empty is a fine answer.
- **Skip list** — one line on what wasn't worth covering.

Do not write an introduction, do not restate this prompt back to me, and do not
end with an offer to elaborate. If a section has nothing in it, omit the
heading entirely rather than writing "None."

## If I ask follow-ups

Stay in the transcripts. If I ask something they don't cover, say so plainly
instead of filling the gap from your own knowledge — I'm trying to find out
what these specific people said, and an answer sourced from elsewhere silently
corrupts that.
